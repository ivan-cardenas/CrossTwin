CrossTwin Map Simplification

A plan to make the map screen easier to use. It borrows from SimCity 4, SimCity (2013) and Cities: Skylines 1 and 2: one topic at a time, data drawn on the map, and expert controls kept out of the way until someone asks for them.

Before and after

What makes the screen hard today

Topics and layers are separate

Clicking a topic button opens its indicators, but its map layers have to be found in the Layers panel, which lists every layer of every app.

Icons without words

The nine topic buttons are emoji with hover-only tooltips. Energy and Green and Blue Space open no panel; Physical Environment's 🪨 is hard to guess.

System counters in prime space

The bottom bar's Layers, Features and Visible counts describe the software, not the city.

Time is set in two places

The year slider sits in the Actions menu and the climate scenario in the Scenarios panel. Each one changes the other.

Calculations live in a generic menu

LST, SVF and Thermal comfort belong to heat, Degree of urbanisation to built-up. They hide under "Actions" next to file upload.

Expert forms open in full

The generator shows 21 inputs at once; Thermal comfort shows 14 weather fields and a 9-field person panel.

Signals are scattered

Stale-result dots, homes without water, site warnings, "trend extrapolation" and the what-if badge each use their own marker in a different place.

Looking and changing are mixed

Editing, the generator and the backup bar share the screen with read-only exploring, so a visitor meets staff tools they cannot use.

What the city builders do

The four games manage the same problem: a city with dozens of interlocking systems, shown to people who are not engineers. Each solved part of it. The green line under each game is what CrossTwin takes from it.

2003 · Maxis

SimCity 4

  • Three separate modes: shaping the land, running the city as mayor, and following individual residents. Tools only appear in the mode they belong to.
  • The most important information sits in one rounded control cluster in the lower left.
  • Data views recolour the buildings themselves; graphs sit in a separate tab.
  • Advisors explain problems and suggest responses, and a news ticker raises issues as they occur.

Take: separate Explore, Plan and Edit modes; one advisor tray for all warnings.

2013 · Maxis

SimCity

  • The interface was modelled on Google Maps and infographics, to make information clearer than in earlier games.
  • Data that used to sit in tables and pop-ups is projected onto the 3D city as data layers.
  • Each building menu has an Info tab (key figures) and a Data Maps tab (its layers), so the numbers and their map are one click apart.
  • The designers aimed to let players read the city from a layer instead of a large spreadsheet.

Take: every indicator card gets "Show on map"; each topic panel has the same tabs.

2015 · Colossal Order

Cities: Skylines

  • 19 info views, which the developers describe in three groups: city services, citizens, and city and map.
  • An info view greys the city and colours only what the view is about.
  • Build categories sit in one bottom toolbar; choosing a building tool can open its info view automatically.
  • Players could toggle info views with a single key.

Take: three topic groups; a topic opens its layers and greys the basemap.

2023–2026 · Colossal Order, then Iceflake

Cities: Skylines II

  • 33 info views behind one icon in the top-left; build menus open the matching info view.
  • Reviewers called the launch interface often confusing, and criticised tutorials delivered as text instead of shown.
  • 2026 patches resized and redrew toolbar icons for clearer meaning, added UI scale and transparency settings, and reworked onboarding.
  • Larger changes ship with a legacy toggle so players can keep the old layout.

Take: label every icon; task-based onboarding; a "classic layout" switch during the change.

Principles

Cities: Skylines, SimCity (2013)

A topic is a lens

Choosing a topic shows its layers, its legend and its panel, and dims everything else. Leaving it restores the previous map.

SimCity (2013)

Numbers point at the map

Each indicator card can show where its value comes from. The side panel holds a few key numbers, not a spreadsheet.

SimCity 4

One mode at a time

Explore is read-only. Plan holds what-ifs and scenarios and writes nothing. Edit (staff) holds the editor, the generator and the restore point.

Cities: Skylines

Few groups, clear words

Three topic groups with labelled icons replace nine emoji. Names follow what people look for, not the app names.

All four

Time is the city clock

Year and climate scenario are one control at the top, like a game's date. Every panel reads it.

Cities: Skylines II, as a warning

Show, then expand

Expert tools open with a preset and three or four fields. "More options" reveals the rest, with the defaults as placeholders.

Steps

Each step ships on its own and leaves the map working. Steps 1 and 2 give most of the gain; 6 and 7 can wait for user feedback.

0

Measure the starting point

  • Time the three tasks below with 3–5 people who have not used CrossTwin (planners, students). Record clicks and where they get stuck.
  • Count overlays open at the end of each task.
No code. A short note in docs/UI_SIMPLIFICATION.md.½ day
1

Declutter without moving anything

  • Replace the emoji with Bootstrap Icons (already loaded) and always-visible short labels.
  • Remove the Layers / Features / Visible counters from the bottom bar.
  • Sentence-case panel titles ("Water supply", not "WATER SUPPLY").
  • Hide topics that open nothing (Energy, Green and blue) until they have a panel, or give them a "No indicators yet" panel with their layers.
mainMap/templates/mainMap.html, core/static/css/mainMap.css, core/static/js/Events.js1–2 days
2

Topics become lenses

  • Add a LENSES table in Config.js: per lens its group, label, icon, layer keys, panel URL and tools.
  • Opening a lens loads its layers, shows only their legends in #legend-stack, and dims the basemap; closing restores the layers the user had on.
  • Group the rail into City systems, People and Environment.
  • Layers panel becomes "Layers in this view" plus an All layers drawer.
core/static/js/Config.js, Layers.js, Panels.js, Map_init.js3–4 days
3

One clock and a status strip

  • Move city name, population, year/scenario chip, weather pill and the advisor flag into one strip at the top of the map.
  • Merge the year picker and scenario choice into the chip; drop the two-way sync between Actions and Scenarios.
  • The bottom bar keeps only the mode switch.
mainMap.js (setActiveYear, syncClimateToYear), Panels.js::showScenarios, WeatherPill.js2–3 days

Same tabs in every lens panel

  • Summary (3–5 headline cards), What-if (sliders), Calculate (the tools that belong to this lens).
  • Each card gets "Show on map", which switches on the layer behind the number.
  • Move the LST, SVF, Thermal comfort and Degree of urbanisation cards into the Calculate tabs; the standalone dashboards render the same partials.

Explore, Plan and Edit modes

  • Explore: read-only, the default for visitors.
  • Plan: what-if sliders and scenarios unlock, with one "What-if" banner across the screen instead of per-card badges.

How to tell it worked

The same three tasks are run before step 1 and after step 7. The click counts are today's minimum path through the current screen.

How hot does my neighbourhood get in 2050?Now: 6 clicks, across the toolbar, Actions menu and Scenarios. Target: 3.
Which homes have no water supply?Now: 2 clicks, but the drops only explain themselves through the legend. Target: 2, with the advisor tray naming the count.
Sketch 300 homes on this field.Now: 4 clicks plus a 21-field form. Target: 4 with a 4-field form.

What not to copy

  • A flat list of 30+ views behind one icon (Cities: Skylines II). Groups first, then views.
  • Text-only tutorials. Show the step on the map.
  • Game styling for its own sake. CrossTwin results are cited in research: units, sources and the "?" help stay one click away.
  • Hiding what-ifs. Plan mode must always show that numbers are a deviation from the stored baseline.

Project rules still apply: JavaScript stays in core/static/js/ with no inline scripts, the side panel and the standalone dashboards keep sharing one partial and one script, new legends go in #legend-stack, and all text stays in English.
